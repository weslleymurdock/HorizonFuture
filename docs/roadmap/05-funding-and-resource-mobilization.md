# 05 — Fundraising, funders, opportunities, grants, and agreements

## Objective
Model how an NGO identifies, requests, receives, and manages financial and non-financial resources from public and private sources.

## Dependencies
Stages 01–04.

## Work
- Model funders and resource providers: individuals, companies, foundations, public bodies, community partners, and crowdfunding/platform sources.
- Model funding sources and their origin/type: unrestricted donations, restricted donations, grants, public/private agreements, sponsorships, fundraising campaigns, recurring contributions, and in-kind contributions.
- Model funding opportunities/calls/editais, eligibility rules, deadlines, required documents, application forms, review stages, scoring criteria, and submission status.
- Model proposals/applications and their requested budgets, attachments, milestones, and decisions.
- Model approved awards/grants and legal instruments/agreements, including parties, purpose, validity, approved value, disbursement schedule, conditions, amendments, deliverables, and reporting obligations.
- Support one project with multiple funding sources and one funding source allocated across multiple projects, with explicit restrictions and allocation records.
- Model expected/received installments, receivables, delays, partial receipts, returns, and closure.
- Distinguish an opportunity, a proposal, an awarded grant, a funding source, and actual cash receipt; do not collapse them into one record.

## Acceptance criteria
- Lifecycle from opportunity discovery through application, approval, agreement, disbursement, execution, reporting, and closure is documented.
- Source restrictions and agreement obligations can be traced to budgets, transactions, and reports.
- Multiple-source and partial-disbursement scenarios are supported.
- Domain glossary and synthetic cases are reviewed by a fundraising/project stakeholder.

## Risks and open decisions
Public-sector instruments and funder-specific reporting rules vary. Preserve configurable requirements and versions rather than hard-coding one edital template.
