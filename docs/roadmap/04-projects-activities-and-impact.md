# 04 — Project lifecycle, activities, indicators, and impact

## Objective
Model the project as the operational and accountability structure that connects strategy, resources, delivery, evidence, and outcomes.

## Dependencies
Stages 01–03.

## Work
- Model project proposals, objectives, target audience, geography, start/end dates, status, responsible unit/team, and lifecycle.
- Model logical frameworks/results chains: objectives, outcomes, outputs, goals, targets, indicators, measurement frequency, baselines, and verification sources.
- Model work plans, milestones, activities, responsible people, schedules, dependencies, risks, and mitigation actions.
- Connect projects to programs, beneficiaries, enrollments, activities, service delivery, funding sources, agreements, and budgets.
- Define evidence records and chronology with provenance, authorship, timestamps, and correction history.
- Define project-level dashboards and reporting dimensions without embedding financial calculations in UI-only logic.

## Acceptance criteria
- A project can be represented from proposal to closure, including plan, execution, evidence, and results.
- Indicators have explicit calculation/measurement semantics and auditable observations.
- Project relationships support multiple funding sources and shared activities without duplicating beneficiaries.
- Synthetic end-to-end examples validate the model.

## Risks and open decisions
Avoid treating activity counts as social impact. Indicator methodology and evidence quality must be explicit and configurable.
